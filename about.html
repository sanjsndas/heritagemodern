About Heritage Modern Spaces

An independent journal exploring the intersection of architectural tradition and contemporary living. We publish long-form guides, design principles, and practical knowledge for people who care about how spaces feel.

Heritage Modern Spaces was founded in 2021 with a simple conviction: that the way we shape our built environment shapes the way we live. We publish in-depth writing on architecture, interior design, materials, and the quiet craft of making a home.

We believe design writing should be useful. Every article is written to be read slowly — with enough depth to inform a real decision, a renovation, or a shift in perspective. We avoid trend lists, shallow roundups, and corporate promotion. What we publish is the result of research, observation, and conversations with architects, makers, and residents.

Our contributors include architects, interior designers, historians, and writers based across Europe and North America. The publication is edited by Elena Marchetti, an architectural historian and former editor at Domus. She lives in a nineteenth-century apartment in Turin that she is slowly — very slowly — renovating.

Editorial Independence

We accept no sponsored content and receive no payment from brands or manufacturers. When we mention products or materials, it is because they are relevant to the subject at hand — not because someone paid for the placement.

“A room is not a container. It is a conversation between light, material, and the person who inhabits it.” — Elena Marchetti, Editor

What We Stand For

01

Depth over speed

We publish less, but we publish with intention. Every piece is researched, considered, and edited for clarity. We would rather write one useful essay than ten shallow ones.

02

Independence

No sponsored posts. No affiliate links. No brand partnerships. Our only obligation is to the reader. This keeps our recommendations honest and our perspective clear.

03

Practical beauty

We care about how things look, but we care more about how they work. The best design is both beautiful and functional — and we write about the intersection of the two.

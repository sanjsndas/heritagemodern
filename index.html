The Quiet Power of Heritage Modernism: When Old Bones Meet New Light

How the most compelling contemporary interiors are those that listen to the past — preserving patina, proportion, and story while introducing clarity and comfort for modern life.

By Elena Marchetti 12 min read March 2026

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I choose between an interior designer and an interior architect? +

Interior designers focus on aesthetics, furnishing, and the sensory experience of a room. Interior architects also address structural changes — moving walls, altering ceiling heights, reconfiguring plumbing and electrical systems. For renovations involving structural work, an interior architect or licensed architect is essential. For styling and furnishing a sound existing space, an interior designer is often the better fit.

What is the single most important principle in space planning? +

Circulation. Every room needs a clear, unobstructed path — ideally 90 centimeters wide in primary routes. Beautiful furniture in a room with poor circulation will always feel wrong. Plan movement first, then place objects. This one principle resolves more design problems than any other.

How much should I budget for a full interior renovation? +

A reasonable planning figure for a mid-range renovation is 800–1,500 USD per square meter, depending on region and scope. Luxury finishes and structural work push this higher. Always reserve 15–20% of your budget for contingencies — unexpected discoveries behind walls are the norm, not the exception.

Are open-plan layouts always better? +

No. Open plan works beautifully for social cooking and family life, but it fails for focused work, quiet reading, and acoustic privacy. The best contemporary layouts are "broken plan" — mostly open, with one or two strategic enclosures (a study, a snug, a utility room) that provide retreat without sacrificing light.

How do I make a small room feel larger? +

Three reliable moves: (1) Keep the floor plane continuous — same flooring throughout, no thresholds. (2) Use large-scale furniture, not small pieces; many small objects create visual clutter. (3) Place the largest piece of furniture against the longest wall to preserve clear sightlines. Mirrors and light colors help, but circulation and sightlines matter more.
